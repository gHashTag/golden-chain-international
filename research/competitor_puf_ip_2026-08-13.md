# Competitive Landscape: Silicon PUF-Based Hardware Root-of-Trust / Key-Generation IP
## As of August 13, 2026

This report covers commercial PUF (Physical Unclonable Function) IP vendors for hardware
root-of-trust and on-chip key generation, verified against each vendor's own website,
datasheet, or a primary-source press release. Where a vendor publishes only marketing
language with no quantitative figures, this is stated explicitly rather than estimated.
All figures below are quoted or closely paraphrased from the cited source; "Not published"
means the field was checked on the vendor's own materials and no number was found there.

## 2. Answers to the Three Specific Questions

### Q1: Does any commercial PUF IP vendor publish a response-bit count and helper-data size for a 128-bit key at a 1e-6 word-error-rate?

**No commercial PUF IP vendor was found that publishes both a response-bit count and a helper-data size specifically tied to a stated 1e-6 word-error-rate for a 128-bit key**, on their own current marketing/datasheet materials. The closest commercial-adjacent figures found are:

- Intrinsic ID's own white paper states "approximately 0.5 KByte [4096 bits] of SRAM PUF response data is needed for a 128-bit key with full randomness" and separately reports a *typical* key-reconstruction error rate of "less than 10^-12" and a worst-case reconstruction failure rate of "lower than 10^-9"  -  but these are not tied together into a single response-bit-count-and-helper-data-size pair at exactly 1e-6 ([Intrinsic ID SRAM PUF white paper](https://data.embeddedcomputing.com/uploads/articles/whitepapers/14491.pdf)).
- Intrinsic ID's DemoKey/BroadKey datasheet gives a concrete **helper-data (Activation Code) size of 480 or 788 bytes**, depending on the configured security strength, for deriving keys from SRAM PUF data  -  this is a genuine commercial-IP figure, but the datasheet does not state the corresponding word-error-rate for that specific configuration ([Renesas-hosted Intrinsic ID DemoKey 2.4 datasheet](https://www.renesas.com/us/en/document/dst/demokey-ds-intrinsic-id)).
- Secure-IC states only "Entropy = 128.0 bit" and "Reliability = fixed to the desired value, e.g., 1 FIT for ASIL D" with no response-bit count or helper-data size given ([Secure-IC PUF IP page](https://www.secure-ic.com/products/securyzr/security-ip/key-management/puf-ip/)).

**The academic/research literature, by contrast, publishes exactly this pairing repeatedly** for a 128-bit key at ~1e-6 failure rate, and these are the closest verifiable numeric answers available:
- A DATE 2018 paper reports that "deriving a 128-bit key with failure rate 10^-6 from SRAM cells with an average BER of 10.22%... requires 1060 raw source bits and 932 [helper data] bits," reducible to 288 bits of helper data with bit-selection and lossless compression ([DATE 2018 conference paper PDF](https://past.date-conference.com/proceedings-archive/2018/pdf/0479.pdf)).
- An IEEE TDSC 2017 paper (Devadas et al., MIT) reports 450-1870 helper-data bits depending on temperature range, for a 128-bit key with "error probability < 10^-6," contrasting against the PUFKY reference design's reported 2052 helper-data bits ([MIT paper, IEEE TDSC](https://people.csail.mit.edu/devadas/pubs/trapdoor.pdf)).
- An IACR ePrint 2015 paper on efficient fuzzy extraction reports concatenated-code parameters (e.g., [255,171,11]-derived codes) achieving expected failure rates around 3.26E-8 to 3.28E-7 for a 128-bit key from a biased PUF, with corresponding PUF-size figures of 1270-13005 bits depending on scheme ([IACR ePrint 2015/854](https://eprint.iacr.org/2015/854.pdf)).
- A CHES 2008 paper on efficient FPGA helper-data algorithms explicitly targets "an error probability of at least 10^-6 ~ 2^-20" and describes hashing 171 error-free SRAM-derived bits down to 128 uniformly random bits ([IACR CHES 2008 paper](https://iacr.org/archive/ches2008/51540179/51540179.pdf)).
- A 2025 arXiv paper on SRAM PUF Hamming-code fuzzy extraction for wearables reports achieving a **128-bit key from 1024 SRAM bits and 896 helper-data bits, with failure probability of 10^-9 or lower**, at a 15% bit-error-probability operating point ([arXiv:2508.07510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07510)).

**Conclusion: this is itself a finding**  -  commercial PUF-IP vendors uniformly omit the response-bit-count/helper-data-size pairing at a stated word-error-rate from public materials (treating it as proprietary implementation detail), while the academic literature treats exactly this trade-off as its central published metric.

### Q2: Does any commercial PUF IP vendor document hashing the helper data into the derived key as a manipulation countermeasure?

**No commercial PUF-IP vendor's own public datasheet or marketing page was found explicitly documenting "hashing the helper data into the derived key" as a named manipulation countermeasure.** Intrinsic ID's white paper describes "privacy amplification" as converting PUF response data (after error correction) into uniformly random keys, but does not frame this as a helper-data-manipulation countermeasure specifically ([Intrinsic ID SRAM PUF white paper](https://data.embeddedcomputing.com/uploads/articles/whitepapers/14491.pdf)). Secure-IC's product page notes only that reliability is "fixed to the desired value" via "adaptive control," with no mention of hashing helper data ([Secure-IC PUF IP page](https://www.secure-ic.com/products/securyzr/security-ip/key-management/puf-ip/)). PUFsecurity's anti-tamper design materials discuss anti-tamper shells and physical/RTL countermeasures but the reviewed public datasheet does not specifically describe hashing helper data as the mechanism ([PUFrt datasheet PDF](https://www.pufsecurity.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/PUFrt_DataSheet_231017.pdf)).

**This technique is well-established and explicitly named in the academic literature**, which is the origin of the idea and where it is documented in detail:
- A TUM dissertation states directly: "The most straightforward strategy... is to hash the secret after error correction with the helper data using a cryptographic hash function. The idea was first suggested in [61] and causes any manipulation of the helper data resulting  -  with high probability  -  in a wrong key" ([TUM dissertation PDF](https://mediatum.ub.tum.de/doc/1740159/1740159.pdf)).
- An IACR TCHES 2021 paper ("When the Decoder Has to Look Twice") explicitly discusses "the efficacy of hashing helper data with the PUF-derived key to prevent [a fault-injection] attack" as one of the countermeasures it evaluates, concluding hashing the helper data with the corrected PUF secret prevents that specific attack only if it is the sole means of forcing the decoder to its correction limit ([IACR ePrint 2021/958](https://eprint.iacr.org/2021/958.pdf)).
- A CNRS/HAL 2021 paper on tamper-resistant PUFs for embedded systems separately discusses write-protection and codeword-masking as the two primary countermeasures against helper-data manipulation, without naming hashing specifically ([CNRS HAL 2021 paper](https://cnrs.hal.science/hal-03783308/file/21_INISCOM_dependable_PUF.pdf)).
- An IACR ePrint 2024 paper on FPGA SRAM PUF implementation explicitly uses "a hashing process... to further enhance the entropy" combined with an additional challenge input, layered on top of LDPC error correction ([IACR ePrint 2024/670](https://eprint.iacr.org/2024/670.pdf)).
- The general fuzzy-commitment construction described in an NSF-funded helper-data-masking paper explicitly notes "a hash value generated from the recovered secret value is compared with a hash value given as part of the helper data" as a verification/tamper-detection step ([NSF PAR paper](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10356837)).

**Conclusion: the hashing-helper-data countermeasure is a documented academic construction with an identified original source and multiple follow-on evaluations, but it is not something any reviewed commercial vendor explicitly names in its own public product documentation**  -  vendors describe tamper resistance in generic terms (anti-tamper shells, adaptive control, physical/RTL countermeasures) without disclosing the specific cryptographic mechanism.

### Q3: Are there open-source or academic-lineage PUF key-generator implementations with published RTL, 2024-2026?

**Yes  -  several, spanning FPGA VHDL/Verilog RTL, an ASIC tapeout, and one recent (2025) academic paper describing (but not itself distributing) a full fuzzy-extractor design:**

- **stnolting/fpga_puf**  -  a technology-agnostic ring-oscillator-based PUF hardware module (VHDL) producing a 96-bit unclonable ID; archived Oct 2023 but remains publicly available as RTL ([GitHub: stnolting/fpga_puf](https://github.com/stnolting/fpga_puf)).
- **litneet64/tt07-RO-based-PUF**  -  a ring-oscillator-based PUF implemented in Verilog for the Sky130 open-source PDK via the Tiny Tapeout program, with 8-bit challenge-response pairs; updated 2024 ([GitHub: litneet64/tt07-RO-based-PUF](https://github.com/litneet64/tt07-RO-based-PUF)).
- **hplp/2025-fpga-design-projects-bowers-lab**  -  a 2025 university course project (University of Virginia ECE 4502) implementing a 128-bit Delay-Difference PUF (DD-PUF) generator in Verilog RTL on FPGA, including SPI interface and FSM control blocks, published April 2025 ([GitHub: hplp/2025-fpga-design-projects-bowers-lab](https://github.com/hplp/2025-fpga-design-projects-bowers-lab)).
- **Dasharo/twpm-fpga-puf**  -  combines the ring-oscillator PUF from stnolting/fpga_puf with a custom arbiter-PUF implementation, targeting Lattice ECP5 hardware with fully open-source Yosys/nextpnr toolchain; updated March 2024 ([GitHub: Dasharo/twpm-fpga-puf](https://github.com/Dasharo/twpm-fpga-puf)).
- **yg99992/SOI_PUF**  -  Verilog/FPGA source code and dataset for a Stagewise-Obfuscated-Interconnection strong PUF, published alongside a 2024 IEEE TIFS paper, hosted on Zenodo (with a companion GitHub link) ([Zenodo record](https://zenodo.org/records/7995053)).
- **Tribler/software-based-PUF**  -  an open-source software-based SRAM PUF (using off-the-shelf SRAM chips, not FPGA fabric) with full enrollment/reconstruction C/C++ source, GPL-3.0 licensed, most recently updated May 2025 ([GitHub: Tribler/software-based-PUF](https://github.com/Tribler/software-based-PUF)).
- A **2025 arXiv paper** from Vietnam National University and University of Genoa describes a complete SRAM-PUF Hamming-code fuzzy-extractor design for energy-harvesting sensors (achieving a 128-bit key from a 1024-bit SRAM block with 896 helper-data bits), evaluated on STM32 Cortex-M0+ hardware, though the paper text as retrieved does not itself state that RTL/source code has been published to a public repository ([arXiv:2508.07510](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2508.07510)).
- Older but foundational **TERO-PUF VHDL** was released with the original 2019 CHES-related "Transient Effect Ring Oscillators Leak Too" paper, explicitly stated as "freely available online... for reproducibility" ([IACR ePrint 2019/300](https://eprint.iacr.org/2019/300.pdf)), and derivative TERO-PUF FPGA work continued through 2023-2025, e.g., a University of Twente 2024/2025 bachelor thesis hosting its RO-PUF design files and simulation code on GitHub ([University of Twente thesis PDF](http://essay.utwente.nl/95895/1/Budnik_BA_EEMCS.pdf)).

**Conclusion: yes, open-source and academic-lineage PUF key-generator RTL is actively published and updated through 2024-2025**, spanning ring-oscillator, arbiter, delay-difference, and SRAM-based designs, hosted on GitHub/Zenodo/GitLab and in several cases tied to peer-reviewed or thesis-level academic work. None of these projects are commercial products, and none report certification (CC EAL / FIPS 140-3 / PSA / SESIP)  -  they are research/education-grade implementations, not production-hardened IP.

## 3. Key Findings on Published-vs-Marketing-Only Disclosure

- **Synopsys PUF (current product page)** publishes no key-length, no error-rate, and no area/gate-count figures at all  -  the page is composed entirely of certification claims and qualitative marketing language (a leadership superlative, paraphrased here because hard rule 9 forbids the word, plus "quantum safe", "any foundry and process-node technology"). This absence is itself the main finding for Synopsys ([Synopsys PUF page](https://www.synopsys.com/designware-ip/security-ip/cryptography-ip/puf/security-puf-ip.html)).
- **Rambus's RT-600 family page** publishes no PUF-specific numeric figures whatsoever (no key length, error rate, or area), stating only that an "embedded PUF" is included among many other RT-600 features; the FIPS/SESIP/PSA claims are made at the level of the whole Root-of-Trust core, not the PUF sub-block specifically ([Rambus Root of Trust page](https://www.rambus.com/security/root-of-trust/)).
- **ICTK's vPUF/VIA PUF** datasheet is almost entirely marketing and standards-compliance language (ISO/IEC 20897, AEC-Q100, JEDEC) with zero numeric technical figures published (no key length, error rate, or area) ([Design & Reuse vPUF page](https://us.design-reuse.com/sip/vpuf-ip-for-roots-of-trust-and-security-chips-ip-51118/)).
- **Analog Devices ChipDNA** materials are consistently qualitative ("excellent reliability," "unprecedented protection," "immune to all known invasive attack tools") across the product landing page, datasheet, and white paper, with no key-length, error-rate, or area figures disclosed at the ChipDNA-block level ([ChipDNA product page](https://www.analog.com/en/lp/001/chipdna-embedded-security-puf-technology.html); [ADI white paper](https://www.farnell.com/datasheets/3164435.pdf)).
- **Intrinsic ID (pre- and post-Synopsys acquisition) is the most quantitatively transparent commercial vendor** reviewed, publishing response-noise percentages, reconstruction error rates, response-size-to-key-size ratios, and helper-data (Activation Code) sizes in bytes in its white papers and datasheets  -  a materially higher disclosure level than any other vendor covered here.
- **PUFsecurity/eMemory discloses the most concrete PUF-core bit width** (1024-bit PUF, four 256-bit fingerprints) of any vendor reviewed, but does not publish a numeric bit-error-rate/word-error-rate figure for key derivation, only a qualitative "lifetime zero BER" claim for its Unique-ID function specifically ([PUFrt datasheet PDF](https://www.pufsecurity.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/10/PUFrt_DataSheet_231017.pdf)).
- **Verayo is effectively defunct** as of the sources reviewed (PitchBook: "Out of Business"; Craft.co: "Closed"), with no current product materials, making any technical comparison moot for 2025-2026 competitive purposes ([PitchBook](https://pitchbook.com/profiles/company/55111-87); [Craft.co](https://craft.co/verayo)).
