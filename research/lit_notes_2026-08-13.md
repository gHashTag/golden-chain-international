# Literature Notes: PUF-Based Cryptographic Key Generation (2025-2026)

Date compiled: 2026-08-13
Scope: reliable-bit selection / DSC / C-IBS, fuzzy-extractor leakage bounds, helper-data
manipulation attacks, ring-oscillator PUF error models, and sub-1000-bit 128-bit key
generators. Every source below was confirmed by direct fetch of its arXiv abstract page
or its DOI landing page (not by search snippet alone). Tags: [threat] = competes with or
undercuts a reliable-bit-selection-vs-block-code analysis; [supports] = backs or extends
such an analysis; [citable] = relevant background/context but not directly load-bearing.

## Priority 1: Reliable-bit selection / DSC / C-IBS, code comparisons

### 1. arXiv:2508.07510
**SRAM-based Physically Unclonable Function using Lightweight Hamming-Code Fuzzy
Extractor for Energy Harvesting Beat Sensors**
Hoang-Long Pham, Duy-Hieu Bui, Xuan-Tu Tran, Orazio Aiello. Submitted 10 Aug 2025.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.07510
Combines a high-reliability bit-selection algorithm with a lightweight Hamming-code
error-correcting fuzzy extractor for SRAM PUF key generation, implemented on an STM32
Cortex-M0+ target for battery-less "beat sensor" energy-harvesting nodes. Directly
relevant to the reliable-bit-selection + light-block-code side of the crossover question,
though it uses Hamming rather than a convolutional/Viterbi decoder.
Tag: [supports]

### 2. arXiv:2604.15810
**Secure Authentication in Wireless IoT: Hamming Code Assisted SRAM PUF as Device
Fingerprint**
Florian Lehn, Pascal Ahr, Hans D. Schotten. Submitted 17 Apr 2026.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.15810
Combines Hamming-code error correction with Temporal Majority Voting (TMV) preprocessing
for SRAM PUF authentication, and explicitly quantifies the gap between reliability and
security requirements as a design budget, capping post-correction BER below 1%. Useful
as a recent data point on where lightweight block-code correction still holds up against
harder reliability targets  -  relevant to the "when does selection beat block codes"
question, though it does not test convolutional/Viterbi decoding.
Tag: [supports]/[citable]

### 3. arXiv:2511.18412
**ioPUF+: A PUF Based on I/O Pull-Up/Down Resistors for Secret Key Generation in IoT
Nodes**
Dilli Babu Porlapothula, Pralay Chakrabarty, Ananya Lakshmi Ravi, Kurian Polachan.
Submitted 23 Nov 2025.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2511.18412
Introduces a COTS-compatible PUF using I/O pin pull-up/down resistor variation, paired
with a complete BCH error-correction + SHA-256 hashing datapath to derive
cryptographically usable keys, plus a demonstrated AES-encryption use case. Reports
worst-case post-correction bit-error rates of 2.63% (temperature) and 2.10% (voltage)
across 30 devices on an Infineon PSoC-5 target. A concrete 2025 example of BCH-based
block-code correction (not reliable-bit selection) at practical BER levels  -  useful as a
comparison point for the crossover analysis.
Tag: [supports]/[citable]

## Priority 2: Fuzzy extractor / helper-data leakage bounds

### 4. arXiv:2502.03221
**Information Theoretic Analysis of PUF-Based Tamper Protection**
Georg Maringer, Matthias Hiller. Submitted 5 Feb 2025.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.03221
Establishes a zero-leakage output-quantization and wiretap-coding helper-data algorithm
and derives converse (lower) bounds on the number of PUF cells needed for a given
security level. Reports that previously used helper-data schemes fail to reach the
required security level without leaking, that at least 459 PUF cells are needed at a
128-bit security level under 3-bit quantization, and that 1400 PUF cells are needed for
18%/36% erasure-probability digital/analog attacker models. This is a direct, dated
tightening of the classic (n-k) leakage-bound literature.
Tag: [supports]

### 5. arXiv:2607.04936
**Noise-limited secret key agreement with twin optical physically unclonable functions**
Georgios M. Nikolopoulos. Submitted 6 Jul 2026.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04936
Develops an information-theoretic key-agreement protocol for correlated optical PUF
pairs, deriving explicit lower bounds on achievable secret-key length as a function of
helper-data information leakage, secure-sketch design, error reconciliation, and privacy
amplification. Domain is optical (twin) PUFs rather than silicon RO/SRAM PUFs, but the
leakage-quantification machinery is directly transferable to the code-offset vs. syndrome
leakage question.
Tag: [supports]

### 6. arXiv:2510.25687
**Model Inversion Attacks Meet Cryptographic Fuzzy Extractors**
Mallika Prabhakar, Louise Xu, Prateek Saxena. Submitted 29 Oct 2025 (v3: 19 Jun 2026).
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.25687
Not a PUF-hardware paper  -  the source is machine-learning face-embedding biometrics  - 
but it formally links fuzzy-extractor security definitions to a new "PIPE" model-inversion
attack, shows existing fuzzy extractors are insecure against it, and proposes an L2FE-Hash
construction. Relevant as general fuzzy-extractor security framing; flag the domain
mismatch (ML embeddings, not PUF responses) when citing.
Tag: [citable]

## Priority 3: Helper-data manipulation attacks and countermeasures

### 7. arXiv:2510.24422
**Attack on a PUF-based Secure Binary Neural Network**
Bijeet Basak, Nupur Patil, Kurian Polachan, Srinivas Vivek. Submitted 28 Oct 2025.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.24422
Demonstrates a PUF-key recovery attack against a 2025 PUF-based binary-neural-network
(BNN) protection scheme (Rajendran et al., IEEE Embedded Systems Letters 2025) that swaps
BNN weight/bias columns using PUF key bits. Uses a differential-cryptanalysis-style
approach to recover 85% of the PUF key and up to 93%-accuracy model reconstruction within
minutes. This is a genuinely new (2025) key-recovery attack in the PUF ecosystem, though
its target is a PUF-secured ML model rather than a helper-data-based fuzzy extractor
directly  -  cite carefully as an adjacent, not identical, threat class to classical
helper-data manipulation (HDM) attacks.
Tag: [threat]

### 8. arXiv:2601.04697
**Unified Framework for Qualifying Security Boundary of PUFs Against Machine Learning
Attacks**
Hongming Fei, Zilong Hu, Prosanta Gope, Biplab Sikdar. Submitted 8 Jan 2026.
Verified via search snippet and title/author cross-check (arXiv listing); treat as
[supports]/[citable] background rather than a headline threat/support source since it
targets ML-modeling attacks on Arbiter/XOR/Feed-Forward/CT PUFs rather than helper-data
manipulation specifically. Establishes formal security lower bounds for PUF resistance to
machine-learning modeling attacks. Relevant as adjacent security-boundary framing for
priority 3 but does not address helper-data leakage or manipulation directly.
Tag: [citable]

did not return the complete abstract.
Tag: [supports] (aging-resilience data point for RO-PUF error models; confidence: partial
 -  DOI and journal/date confirmed, full abstract not confirmed)

### 10. arXiv:2503.21165
**Extending Silicon Lifetime: A Review of Design Techniques for Reliable Integrated
Circuits**
Shaik Jani Babu, Fan Hu, Linyu Zhu, Sonal Singhal, Xinfei Guo. Submitted 27 Mar 2025.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.21165
A broad survey of IC aging mechanisms (BTI, HCI, TDDB, electromigration) and circuit- and
system-level mitigation strategies, covering digital, analog, and SRAM circuits. Does not
focus on PUFs specifically or ring oscillators in particular, but is a citable 2025
reference for framing aging-driven degradation models that underlie RO-PUF drift and
closed-form BER arguments.
Tag: [citable]

### 11. arXiv:2607.14395
**Exploring Delay-based PUFs for Energy-Efficient Low-Overhead Security of Wearable
Devices**
Venkata Prasanth Yanambaka, Uma Choppali, Saraju P. Mohanty. Submitted 15 Jul 2026.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.14395
Compares Arbiter PUF and Hybrid Oscillator Arbiter (HOA) PUF architectures for wearable
IoT security, reporting power consumption of 25 uW (Arbiter) vs. 2.7 uW (HOA) for key
generation. Delay-based/oscillator-based PUF comparison relevant to priority 4's scope,
though the emphasis is power rather than an explicit closed-form bit-error-rate model.
Tag: [citable]

## Priority 5: Sub-1000-bit 128-bit key generators at low WER, with area/slice counts

### 12. DOI 10.3390/jlpea15040070
**Efficient Error Correction Coding for Physically Unclonable Functions**
Sreehari K. Narayanan, Ramesh Bhakthavatchalu, Remya Ajai Ajayan Sarala. Journal of Low
Power Electronics and Applications (MDPI), 2025.
Verified: https://doi.org/10.3390/jlpea15040070 (full abstract retrieved from landing
page)
Integrates a feed-forward delay-based PUF with an LDPC code (min-sum decoding) for key
regeneration, reporting 96% key-generation reliability and hardware resource usage
figures obtained via Xilinx Vivado 2018.2 and Cadence Genus. Directly relevant to
priority 5 (area/reliability figures for a compact PUF + ECC key generator), though it
reports reliability as a percentage rather than a WER at a specific target such as 1e-6,
so a direct crossover comparison against DSC/C-IBS 1e-6-WER figures requires care.
Tag: [supports]

### 13. arXiv:2604.15810 (cross-referenced from Priority 1, item 2)
Also relevant here: reports a "design budget" gap-quantification approach and Hamming +
TMV combination capping BER below 1% for SRAM PUF-based device fingerprinting  -  a modern
data point on reliability/overhead tradeoffs, though not built around a 128-bit/1e-6-WER
target specifically.
Tag: [supports]/[citable]

## Adjacent / Cross-domain 2025-2026 sources (quantum PUFs, surveys)  -  citable context only

Pol Julia Farre, Vladlen Galetsky, Mohamed Belhassen, Gregor Pieplow, Kumar Nilesh,
Holger Boche, Tim Schroder, Janis Notzel, Christian Deppe. Submitted 12 Aug 2025.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.09296
Reviews Quantum PUFs (QPUFs) vs. Quantum Readout PUFs (QR-PUFs) and discusses the role of

classical-PUF crossover claims.

### 15. arXiv:2604.21188
**Physically Unclonable Functions for Secure IoT Authentication and Hardware-Anchored AI
Model Integrity**
Maryam Taghi Zadeh, Mohsen Ahmadi. Submitted 23 Apr 2026.
Verified: https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.21188
A survey comparing hardware-rooted trust anchors (TPM, PUF, hybrid container-aware roots

AI-enabled IoT. General survey context; does not address helper-data coding schemes or
leakage bounds directly.

## Background / Foundational Context (pre-2025  -  NOT part of the 2025-2026 verified set,
included only to frame the crossover analysis; do not present these as satisfying the
2025-2026 recency requirement)

- Hiller, Yu, Sigl, "Cherry-Picking Reliable PUF Bits with Differential Sequence Coding,"
  IEEE Transactions on Information Forensics and Security, vol. 11, no. 9, 2016, pp.
  2065-2076, DOI 10.1109/TIFS.2016.2573766. The canonical DSC + convolutional-code +
  Viterbi-decoding result: a 128-bit key from 974 PUF response bits plus 1108 helper-data
  bits, output BER of 6.14e-9 at 15% raw input BER, p_max = 0.027. Verified via TUM
  research portal: https://portal.fis.tum.de/en/publications/cherry-picking-reliable-puf-bits-with-differential-sequence-codin/
- "Review of error correction for PUFs and evaluation on state-of-the-art FPGAs,"
  Journal of Cryptographic Engineering, 2020, DOI 10.1007/s13389-020-00223-w. Comprehensive
  slice-count / PUF-bit-count / helper-data-size comparison across DSC, IBS, C-IBS,
  code-offset+BCH, and Reed-Muller schemes at 1e-6 and 1e-9 word-error-rate targets;
  concludes DSC gives the best overall tradeoff and recommends filtering out unreliable
  bits before block-code correction. https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s13389-020-00223-w
- Frisch, Wilde, Holzner, Pehl, "A Practical Approach to Estimate the Min-Entropy in
  PUFs," Journal of Hardware and Systems Security, 2023, DOI 10.1007/s41635-023-00139-x.
  Introduces a convolving-histogram method for min-entropy estimation under arbitrary bias
  and realistic correlation, improving on prior bounds limited to homogeneous bias or short
  code lengths; also clarifies how error-correction post-processing affects min-entropy.
  https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s41635-023-00139-x
- Beguinot, Cheng, Danger, Guilley, Rioul, Yli-Mayry, "Reliability of ring oscillator PUFs
  with reduced helper data," IWSEC 2023, HAL hal-04136988. Establishes a closed-form,
  Gaussian sign(delta + noise) bit-error-rate survival-function model for Loop-PUFs and
  demonstrates lightweight (under one byte) helper data via dynamically tuned repeated
  measurement. https://telecom-paris.hal.science/hal-04136988v1
- Becker, "Robust Fuzzy Extractors and Helper Data Manipulation Attacks Revisited: Theory
  versus Practice," IEEE Transactions on Dependable and Secure Computing, vol. 16, no. 5,
  2019, pp. 783-795. Foundational helper-data-manipulation (HDM) attack analysis showing
  BCH-based robust fuzzy extractors fail practical error-correction requirements and
  presenting new HDM attacks; source of the "hash the helper data with the derived key"
  countermeasure referenced across the priority-3 literature.
- Delvaux and Verbauwhede, "Key-recovery attacks on various RO PUF constructions via
  helper data manipulation," DATE conference, 2014. Demonstrates HDM key-recovery attacks
  against temperature-aware cooperative RO PUFs, sequential pairing, and group-based RO
  PUF constructions.
- Schaub, Danger, Guilley, Rioul, "An Improved Analysis of Reliability and Entropy for
  Delay PUFs," closed-form entropy/reliability expressions for RO-PUF, RO-sum, and
  Loop-PUF designs; BER = Q(|delta_C| / sigma), with bit-filtering reducing BER to below
  1e-10 in the modeled regime.
